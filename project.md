# Research Project

## Project Overview

This project explores how safety features on dating platforms can be systematically identified, categorized, and evaluated. Building on prior platform auditing work, the research adapts existing safety evaluation methodologies to the context of online dating, where users face unique risks related to identity, communication, privacy, and interpersonal interactions.

The project is being conducted under the guidance of Dr. Laura Edelson as part of the NSF DREAM (Distributed Research Experiences for Advanced Master's) program.

## Why This Research Matters

Dating platforms have become an increasingly important way for people to meet, form relationships, and build community. At the same time, these platforms expose users to a variety of risks, including unwanted contact, harassment, inappropriate content, image misuse, coercive behavior, and identity deception.

While many dating platforms publicly promote safety tools and trust-and-safety initiatives, there is limited research examining how these systems are designed, communicated, and implemented in practice. Developing more systematic approaches to evaluating safety systems can help researchers better understand how platforms manage risk and support users.

## Research Questions

This project is guided by several overarching questions:

- What safety features are available on major dating platforms?
- How do platforms communicate safety resources and interventions to users?
- How can safety features be categorized in a consistent and reproducible way?
- What risks are these features designed to address?
- How can existing platform auditing methodologies be adapted to evaluate dating-platform safety systems?
- What platform characteristics may require different forms of intervention, monitoring, or protective infrastructure?

## Methodology

The project combines literature review, feature inventory development, document analysis, and auditing methodology design.

Key activities include:

- Reviewing existing literature on online safety, trust and safety systems, platform governance, and user experiences on dating platforms.
- Developing a taxonomy of platform risks and corresponding safety interventions.
- Identifying and documenting publicly available safety features across dating platforms.
- Analyzing safety hubs, help centers, platform documentation, and other public-facing resources.
- Adapting elements of prior platform auditing methodologies to the dating-app context.
- Developing structured testing scenarios and account configurations for future platform assessments.

The current phase of the project focuses primarily on Tinder and Bumble, allowing for a more reproducible feature-sourcing and evaluation process before expanding to additional platforms.

## Preliminary Observations

Several themes have emerged during the early stages of the project.

First, defining what constitutes a "safety feature" is often less straightforward than expected. Safety interventions may appear as user-facing tools, reporting systems, educational resources, moderation mechanisms, verification programs, or platform policies.

Second, some privacy and safety-related capabilities are offered through premium subscription tiers. Features such as profile visibility controls, advanced filtering options, and enhanced communication tools raise questions about the relationship between monetization, privacy, and safety.

Finally, many platform safety systems rely on forms of verification, identity confirmation, or user monitoring. This raises broader questions about how platforms define trust, risk, and safety, as well as how these systems may affect different user communities.

## Next Steps

The next phase of the project will focus on:

- Refining the safety-feature taxonomy.
- Expanding feature inventories and documentation.
- Conducting deeper analysis of platform safety resources and documentation.
- Developing testing scenarios and account configurations for future assessments.
- Exploring how factors such as profile verification, identity verification, account characteristics, and subscription status should be incorporated into evaluation frameworks.
- Building a structured methodology that can support future comparisons across dating platforms.
