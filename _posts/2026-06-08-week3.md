This week focused on strengthening the project's documentation, refining the feature categorization framework, and continuing the analysis of premium safety and privacy features. As the feature inventory expanded, I spent more time documenting methodological decisions and ensuring that the approach could be applied consistently across platforms. This process reinforced the importance of slowing down, carefully documenting assumptions, and building research methods that others can understand and reproduce.

## Activities

- Continued sourcing and documenting safety features across Tinder and Bumble.
- Refined the project's feature taxonomy and categorization framework.
- Expanded analysis of premium safety and privacy features.
- Reviewed and documented feature inclusion criteria and categorization decisions.
- Continued adapting elements of the Instagram Teen Accounts auditing methodology to the dating-app context.
- Discussed opportunities to align feature categorization and future testing approaches with another student interested in this area of research.

## Findings

As the feature inventory grew, I found that categorization often required more interpretation than expected. Many features serve multiple purposes simultaneously, functioning as privacy tools, safety interventions, trust signals, or engagement mechanisms depending on how they are implemented and used. This reinforced the importance of establishing clear definitions and documentation for each category.

I also continued exploring the relationship between premium subscriptions and safety. Features such as Incognito Mode, profile visibility controls, advanced filtering options, and pre-match communication tools highlighted how some forms of privacy management and risk reduction may be bundled into paid subscription offerings. While these features are often marketed as premium experiences, they can also influence users' ability to manage interactions and control visibility on the platform.

Another important realization was that adapting an existing methodology requires more than replicating its structure. The Instagram Teen Accounts report provides a useful foundation, but some of the aspects need to be reconsidered in the dating-app context. This week involved thinking more critically about which components can be transferred directly and which require modification, specifically around the the "supervisory" role initially seen in the Teens experience.

As I spent more time reviewing platform safety systems, I also became increasingly interested in content-based safety interventions. Features such as "Are You Sure?" prompts, content warnings, and other friction-based mechanisms raise questions about how platforms determine what content should trigger intervention. These systems appear to rely on assumptions about language, context, and harm, which may vary significantly across cultures, identities, and communities. While this remains outside the current scope of the project, it is an area I hope to explore further as the research develops.

## Challenges

- Maintaining consistency as feature categories became more detailed and nuanced.
- Balancing simplicity and comprehensiveness when refining the feature taxonomy.
- Documenting methodological decisions in a way that supports transparency and reproducibility.
- Determining how concepts from social media auditing translate to dating platforms.
- Resisting the temptation to expand the project's scope before establishing a strong methodological foundation.

This week also reinforced the importance of building research processes that can be understood and applied consistently by others. As the project moves toward testing scenario development, I have been thinking more about how future work could be structured to support shared feature reviews, independent validation, and testing scenarios that require interactions between multiple users or accounts.

- Continue refining and validating the feature taxonomy.
- Expand documentation of feature sourcing methods and categorization decisions.
- Develop testing scenarios, account configurations, and evaluation criteria that will support future assessments of platform safety features.
- Explore how factors such as profile verification, identity verification, account characteristics, and subscription status should be incorporated into future testing scenarios.
- Continue adapting and documenting the auditing methodology for the dating-app context.
