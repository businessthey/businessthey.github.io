This week focused on developing a deeper understanding of the online safety research landscape and the methodologies that may inform this project. Much of my time was spent reviewing prior work on platform safety, auditing approaches, and dating-app safety interventions. In parallel, I relocated across the country to begin a Product Management internship at T-Mobile, which provided an unexpected lesson in prioritization and balancing multiple commitments.

## Activities

- Conducted a literature review on online safety, trust and safety systems, and platform auditing methodologies.
- Read *Teen Accounts, Broken Promises: How Instagram is Failing to Protect Minors* and reviewed the report's testing scenarios and evaluation framework.
- Read *Analyzing Social Media Claims regarding Youth Online Safety Features to Identify Problem Areas and Communication Gaps*.
- Read *Individualized Responsibility, Policing, and Surveillance: Examining the Carceral Logic in Dating App Companies' Approaches to Safety*.
- Reviewed prior work from my advisor to better understand how platform safety features were identified, categorized, and evaluated.
- Began exploring potential sources for identifying dating-app safety features, including platform websites, safety centers, help documentation, transparency reports, and public-facing communications.
- Relocated to Seattle and began onboarding for a summer Product Management internship at T-Mobile.

## Findings

One of the most useful insights from this week's reading was the distinction between a platform's public claims about safety and the actual behavior of its safety systems. The Instagram Teen Accounts report demonstrated how structured testing scenarios can be used to evaluate whether safety features function as advertised.

I also began thinking more critically about what constitutes a "feature" for the purposes of this research. Initially, I assumed features would primarily consist of user-facing tools visible within an application's interface. However, the literature and prior audits suggest that safety interventions often span reporting mechanisms, educational resources, moderation systems, account controls, verification tools, and platform policies. As a result, defining a clear and consistent feature taxonomy will be an important part of the project.

The dating-app safety literature further highlighted how platform approaches to safety can shape user experiences and expectations, reinforcing the importance of examining both technical tools and broader platform practices.

## Challenges

A primary challenge this week was determining how to define the scope of the feature inventory. Safety interventions appear across many different surfaces and documentation sources, making it difficult to determine what should be included and how categories should be structured.

Another challenge was balancing research progress alongside a cross-country move and the start of a new internship. While the transition required significant time and attention, it also reinforced the importance of prioritization and maintaining momentum on longer-term research goals.

## Next Steps

- Continue reviewing literature related to platform safety and auditing methodologies.
- Develop an initial framework for defining and categorizing dating-app safety features.
- Identify candidate platforms for evaluation.
- Begin documenting potential feature sources and collection methods.
- Refine research questions and determine how existing auditing approaches can be adapted to the dating-app context.
