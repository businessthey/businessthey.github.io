This week focused on moving from feature documentation toward preparing for direct platform testing. After several weeks of identifying and categorizing safety features, I began working through what would actually be required to evaluate these systems in practice. I also met with my advisor to confirm the overall approach and discuss how to structure the next phase of the research.

## Activities

- Reviewed the developing feature inventory and identified features that could potentially be evaluated through direct platform testing.
- Began designing controlled test accounts and documenting the account characteristics needed for different testing scenarios.
- Documented platform onboarding requirements and considered which account attributes may affect access to safety features.
- Mapped individual safety features to their testing prerequisites, including whether they require matching, messaging, verification, specific account configurations, or interactions between accounts.
- Began distinguishing features that can be evaluated through document analysis from those that require direct testing.
- Met with my advisor to review the proposed testing approach and confirm the direction of the next phase of the project.

## Findings

Preparing for testing made it clear that evaluating safety features requires more than simply creating accounts and checking whether a feature exists. Different features depend on different platform states and user interactions. Some are available immediately, while others require users to match, exchange messages, complete verification, change visibility settings, or meet other conditions before they can be observed.

Account configuration also emerged as an important methodological consideration. During onboarding, platforms collect or request information such as gender, dating preferences, location preferences, photos, and optional profile characteristics. Some safety and privacy tools also depend on verification status or other account-level settings. These differences need to be documented so that testing conditions can be reproduced consistently.

I also began mapping features to the accounts, interactions, and prerequisites required to test them. This helped turn the feature inventory into the beginnings of a structured testing protocol rather than a collection of individual platform features.

## Challenges

One of the main challenges was determining how to create realistic testing scenarios while maintaining controlled and repeatable conditions. Dating-platform features are highly dependent on interactions between users, which means testing one feature may require several preceding actions before the relevant safety intervention becomes available.

Another challenge was identifying which account characteristics should be treated as experimental variables and which should remain consistent across tests. Decisions around verification status, profile characteristics, location, subscription status, and previous interactions could potentially influence how a platform behaves.

This stage also highlighted practical constraints around account creation and platform access. These limitations reinforced the need to document testing conditions carefully and prioritize scenarios that can be evaluated reliably.

## Next Steps

- Finalize the initial set of controlled test accounts and document their configurations.
- Continue mapping safety features to the account states and interactions required for testing.
- Develop structured test cases for the first set of safety features.
- Define which variables should remain constant across tests and which should be intentionally varied.
- Establish criteria for documenting expected versus observed platform behavior.
- Begin conducting initial tests once the testing protocol and account configurations are finalized.
